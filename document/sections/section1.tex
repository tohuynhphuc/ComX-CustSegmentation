\section{Project Context and Data Source}
In modern retail, analyzing customer purchasing behavior is critical for making effective business decisions. Traditional transaction tables store raw order data, but they do not automatically reveal which customers purchase repeatedly, which generate the highest value, or which groups require targeted marketing strategies. 

This project analyzes \textbf{CompanyX}, a global retailer specializing in bicycles, accessories, and apparel. The transactional database records customer demographics, sales orders, product lines, and order details from May 2011 through June 2014. To provide a consistent baseline for analysis, 30 June 2014 is selected as the primary historical cutoff date for the reporting dashboards.

The project focuses on \emph{customer-centric analytics} rather than basic sales reporting. Specifically, our solution combines Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) modeling, unsupervised K-Means clustering, and a dimensional data warehouse to segment customers into actionable groups. An automated ETL pipeline keeps customer profiles up to date and re-clusters buyers near-real-time after new transactions, while also storing monthly snapshots to track long-term behavioral trends.

\section{Business Problem}
Retail managers need to know not only overall sales figures, but also \emph{which customer groups drive revenue and how their purchasing habits evolve over time}. Four key business challenges motivated this project:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Customer Retention and Inactivity:} Identifying one-time buyers versus loyal repeat customers is essential. Without tracking purchase frequency and the elapsed time since the last order, marketing teams cannot easily detect customer attrition.
  \item \textbf{Uneven Spending Distribution:} A small proportion of high-value buyers often accounts for the majority of company revenue. Treating all customers identically leads to wasted marketing budgets and missed VIP loyalty opportunities.
  \item \textbf{Cross-Selling and Up-Selling Opportunities:} Understanding a customer's spending tier and engagement stage helps the sales team recommend complementary items, such as bike accessories and apparel, at the right moment.
  \item \textbf{Operational vs. Historical Analysis:} An operational view updated immediately after a purchase answers different questions than a standardized month-end snapshot. Both views are required to manage immediate transaction events while tracking multi-year customer migration.
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives and Deliverables}
The project delivers an end-to-end Business Intelligence solution that combines \textbf{data mining customer segmentation} with an \textbf{automated data pipeline}. The core technical objectives are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Design and implement a customer-focused dimensional data warehouse featuring Type 0, Type 1, and Type 2 Slowly Changing Dimensions (SCD);
  \item Build an automated SSIS ETL pipeline with incremental change detection to load customer profiles efficiently;
  \item Implement a near-real-time operational scoring path that recalculates RFM metrics and assigns clusters following new purchases;
  \item Construct a monthly periodic snapshot process to capture historical customer behavior and support trend analytics;
  \item Train, validate, and version K-Means clustering models in Python, persisting cluster centroids and scaling parameters in the warehouse;
  \item Deliver an interactive, multi-page Power BI dashboard that translates analytical findings into practical business strategies.
\end{enumerate}

The delivered artifacts include the SQL Server warehouse schema, deployed SSIS ETL packages, Python machine learning scripts, an Apache Airflow retraining DAG, and a comprehensive Power BI dashboard.
